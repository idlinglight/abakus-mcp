# ADR-0007: calc: one expression, exact fractions, tallies by name

Status: accepted, 2026-11-08; amended 2026-11-08 (underflow and the readable policy in full, when `fraction` is
left out, `{name:value}`, unknown names, rounded tallies in the response, errors while evaluating, parser details;
see the end)

## Context

Agents need formulas, not only sums: an error rate from two tallies, the share of a budget that is left, an
amount after ten years of growth. Worked out in the head, digits go wrong; `bc` truncates divisions
([ADR-0005](0005-numbers.md)); a script is the ad-hoc tooling abakus exists to save.

Two other shapes were considered and rejected:

- Operator tools (`multiply`, `divide` as separate calls): the formula becomes the order of the calls, which
  nobody can read back; a call made twice or in the wrong order corrupts the result without a sign; every
  operation costs a round trip.
- A calculator with one unnamed "display" value: state that every session collides on. Give it a name and it
  is a tally ([ADR-0004](0004-tallies.md)).

Tally names contain `-` and `.` and may start with a digit (`errors-k3f9`, `2026-11-08`), so a bare tally name
in an expression cannot be told from a subtraction or a number.

Before deciding, three drafts of the tool description, the same except for how an expression refers to a
tally, were given to nine fresh agents (Haiku, Sonnet and Opus for each draft) with nine tasks, some of them
traps (`-20` squared, "19% VAT", "1,234,567 rows in 2.5 h", shared tallies without a suffix):

- (A) the full tally name in braces: `{errors-k3f9} / {requests-p2x7} * 100`;
- (B) lowercase short names, bound in a `tallies` argument: `errors / requests * 100` with
  `{"errors": "errors-k3f9", "requests": "requests-p2x7"}`;
- (C) the same with uppercase short names, which can never be tally names: `ERRORS / REQUESTS * 100`.

All 81 calls were right for their draft, and no agent fell into a trap. The drafts differed in what agents had
to guess: under (B) two of three blurred or asked whether a short name may equal a tally name
(`"builds": "builds"`), and where `:count` goes; under (C) nobody did. Under every draft, agents asked whether
shared tallies work the same way, whether the space after a comma is required, whether suffixes are
case-sensitive, whether `round` keeps trailing zeros and whether intermediate results are rounded. Two noticed
that the server's instructions ("numbers may be given as strings such as 1,234.5") contradict the expression
rules. No agent wrote `×` or `÷`.

## Decision

### The tool

`calc` `{expression}` works out one expression and returns one result. It is stateless and read-only: it reads
tallies and never changes them. Unknown arguments are errors, as in every tool.

### Tally references

- `{name}` stands for a tally's value, `{name:count}` for its count. Any tally, shared ones included, by its
  full name. Inside the braces `-` and `.` belong to the name; `:` cannot occur in a tally name, so `:count`
  is unambiguous. (The amendment at the end adds `{name:value}`.)
- All tallies an expression uses are read under one lock, so their values come from the same moment.
- An unknown name is the error the tally tools give (with `details.matching` and `details.existing`), and
  nothing is computed. (The amendment at the end gives its advice for `calc`.)
- A tally that says `exact: false` makes the result `exact: false`: its value was rounded once already. (How
  that shows in the response: see the amendment at the end.)
- Short names bound in an argument (B, C) read well and would let one formula run over other tallies, but they
  add a second argument and a second kind of name that has to agree with the first, and the probe found no
  gain in accuracy. They stay possible later: uppercase names are unused in this grammar.

### Numbers

- Literals as elsewhere in abakus, without grouping: `12`, `0.5`, `.5`, `2.5e3`, or one suffix `k` or `K`
  (thousand), `M`, `G`, `T`; a lowercase `m` is refused. They may stand anywhere a number goes, inside
  parentheses and function calls too. The decimal mark is always `.`, and there is no `decimal` argument.
- No thousands separators. A comma directly between two digits is refused as one, so function arguments are
  separated by a comma and a space: `round(x, 2)`. `max(1,234)` is an error instead of the maximum of 1
  and 234.
- The limits of ADR-0005 apply to each literal: at most 38 significant digits, below 10^165, no digits below
  10^-128.
- No `_` between digits, no constants (`pi`, `e`), no units.

### Operators

- `+`, `-` (also `−`), `*` (also `×` and `·`), `/` (also `÷`), `^` (also `**`), parentheses, a leading `-`
  or `+`. Whitespace between tokens is ignored.
- Precedence as in mathematics: `^` binds tighter than a leading minus and groups from the right (`-2^2` is
  -4, `(-2)^2` is 4, `2^3^2` is 512); then `*` and `/`, then `+` and `-`, each from the left. `2^-1` is
  allowed.
- No implicit multiplication: `2(3+4)` and `2{errors-k3f9}` are errors that show where the `*` goes.
- `%` is an error. Its message says to multiply by 100 for a percentage, or by 1.1 to add 10%. It is not
  modulo either: the two meanings are why it is refused.

### Powers and functions

- The exponent of `^` must be a whole number from -9999 to 9999; the base can be any value. `0^0` is 1, the
  empty product. 0 to a negative power is a division by zero. A fractional exponent is an error that says
  roots are not supported.
- Functions: `abs(x)`, `round(x, places)` (half away from zero; places a whole number from -165 to 128, as in
  `DecimalMath`), `min(a, b, …)`, `max(a, b, …)`. Names are lowercase.
- No square root and no fractional powers in this version (see below).

### Exactness

- The expression is computed with exact fractions, an integer numerator over an integer denominator, and is
  never rounded on the way. The result is rounded once, at the end: `1/3*3` is 1, and `1000 * 1.045^10` is
  exact. Operator order no longer matters for exactness, and no rule about rounding between steps is needed.
- The result is exact when it is a terminating decimal of at most 38 significant digits. Otherwise it follows
  the readable policy of ADR-0005: 15 significant digits, half away from zero, with `exact: false`. The
  response then adds `fraction`, the exact value as `"numerator/denominator"`, so the
  rounding loses nothing an agent might need. (The amendment at the end states the policy in full, whole units
  and the 10^-128 step included, and leaves `fraction` out beyond 100 digits.)
- `round(x, places)` rounds the exact value. Trailing zeros are not kept: `round(12.5, 2)` is 12.5.
- A result of 10^165 or more is the overflow error of ADR-0005. A non-zero result that rounds to 0 at
  10^-128, that is below half of 10^-128, is its underflow error; from half of 10^-128 up, the result is
  10^-128 with `exact: false`. (Corrected in the amendment at the end; this said every non-zero result below
  10^-128.) Intermediate values may go beyond that range, within the limits below.
- The arithmetic builds on the existing exact core (`BigUInt`, `ExactDecimal`); Foundation's `Decimal`
  arithmetic stays unused.

### Limits

Each limit is an error that names it and says what to do instead:

- the expression has at most 1000 characters;
- parentheses, function calls, powers and leading minus signs nest at most 64 deep;
- the numerator and the denominator of every value an operation produces have at most 2000 digits. Size is
  estimated before a power is computed, so `9^9999^9999` is refused at once instead of after a long
  computation.

The limits keep one call fast and stop a malformed expression from crashing the server for the whole session
(a recursive parser without a depth limit runs out of stack).

### Response

`result` is a JSON number. `read` is the expression as it was understood, with parentheses for every
operation except the outermost one; `tallies` lists each reference with the number used (left out when there
are none); `exact: false` and `fraction` appear only when the result was rounded. (Amended at the end:
`exact: false` also appears for a rounded tally, `fraction` only for a rounding of the result itself.)

```json
{"expression": "{errors-k3f9} / {requests-p2x7} * 100"}
→ {"result": 2.96, "read": "({errors-k3f9} / {requests-p2x7}) * 100",
   "tallies": [{"name": "errors-k3f9", "of": "value", "value": 37},
               {"name": "requests-p2x7", "of": "value", "value": 1250}]}

{"expression": "1/3"}
→ {"result": 0.333333333333333, "exact": false, "fraction": "1/3", "read": "1 / 3"}
```

A syntax error gives the position (characters counted from 1) and the expression, says what was expected
there, and says what to write instead: `Unexpected "%" at character 11 of "37 / 1250 %": there is no %
operator, and it is not modulo either. For a percentage, multiply by 100 (37 / 1250 * 100); to add 10%,
multiply by 1.1.` Errors found while evaluating read the same way (see the amendment at the end).

### Texts for agents

- The description states the rules above in the probe's short form and answers the probe's questions: shared
  tallies work the same way; the space after a comma is required; which suffixes exist and that `m` is
  refused; trailing zeros are dropped; nothing is rounded before the result; the result is a JSON number; an
  unknown tally is an error.
- The server's instructions mention `calc`, and say that strings such as "1,234.5" are for number arguments,
  while expressions take plain numbers.

### Command line

`abakus calc <expression>…` joins its arguments with spaces and prints the JSON. The README says to quote the
expression, since `*`, `(`, `{` and `^` mean something to the shell.

## Consequences

- "Collect now, decide the formula later" works: tallies collect, `calc` combines them.
- Every result is exact or rounded exactly once from an exact value, and says which.
- Exact fractions cost size, which the limits bound. Expressions agents write in practice stay far below them.
- New functions, constants or operators amend this ADR together with the description.

## Considered and not chosen

- A square root in this version. A root is irrational in general and cannot stay an exact fraction. Computed to
  a fixed number of digits, it can come out wrong in all 15 digits shown: in `sqrt(10^60 + 1) - 10^30` the
  difference cancels every digit of a 40-digit root. Correct digits need an interval, a lower and an upper
  bound carried through every operation, with more digits until both bounds round to the same result and an
  error if that takes too many. Left out until a use needs it; adding it amends this ADR.
- `-2^2` as an error asking for `(-2)^2` or `-(2^2)`, because Excel and `bc` read it as 4. Mathematics, Python
  and the agents in the probe read it as -4, and `read` shows the reading.
- The full 38 digits for a rounded result: harder to read, and `fraction` already keeps the exact value.

## Amendment 2026-11-08: details settled while building calc

### Context

The exact arithmetic, the parser and the tool settled details that the decision above left open or stated
differently: how far ADR-0005's readable policy applies, where underflow starts, when `fraction` is left out,
how a tally's rounded value and an unknown name show, and what the errors found while evaluating say.

### Decision

The result:

- The readable policy is ADR-0005's in full. A result is exact when it is a terminating decimal of at most 38
  significant digits whose last digit is at 10^-128 or above (`3e-128 / 2` is 2e-128 with `exact: false`).
  Otherwise it is rounded half away from zero to 15 significant digits, or to whole units when the integer part
  has more than 15 digits, 38 digits at most (`10^20 / 3` is 33333333333333333333).
- Underflow as in ADR-0005: a value from half of 10^-128 up rounds to 10^-128 with `exact: false`; only a
  non-zero value that rounds to 0 is the underflow error. This corrects the decision above, which made every
  non-zero result below 10^-128 an error.
- The overflow and underflow errors use ADR-0005's wording and add how to see the digits: divide or multiply
  the expression by a power of ten such as `1e100`.
- `fraction` appears only when the numerator and the denominator have at most 100 digits each; beyond that it
  is left out, and the result still says `exact: false`. The description says so in a short clause. A whole
  number rounded because it has more than 38 digits gives its digits alone, without `/1` (`2^200`).
- The 2000-digit limit applies to every reduced value an operation produces; a product inside one operation
  may briefly be larger.

Tallies:

- `{name:value}` is accepted and means `{name}`; `read` writes `{name}`. `:count` and `:value` are lowercase
  only, as names are.
- The tally store is opened only when the expression has references, so an expression without any never
  touches the state folder. The tallies are read under one shared lock.
- An unknown name is the tally tools' error with advice of its own (`UnknownNameUse.read`, beside `.change`
  and `.drop`): the tally may never have been created, may have been dropped or may have expired; nothing was
  computed. It never suggests creating a tally, which would not give the formula a value. When tallies with
  the name plus a suffix exist, it says they may belong to other sessions, and to write the full name, suffix
  included, only for the one the agent knows it means (comparing created times). The first unknown name in
  the text is the one reported.
- An unreadable tally file is the tally tools' error for it, which names the tally and its file, followed by
  "Nothing was computed."
- A tally with `exact: false` used for its value makes the result `exact: false`, and its entry in `tallies`
  says `"exact": false`. `fraction` then appears only when the rounding of the result itself dropped digits:
  it undoes that rounding, not the tally's. A count is always exact, so `{name:count}` of such a tally leaves
  the result exact.

The response and its errors:

- Keys in this order: `result`, `exact` (only `false`), `fraction`, `read`, `tallies`. `tallies` lists each
  distinct reference, a name with `of: "value"` or `"count"`, once, in the order of first appearance, as
  `{"name", "of", "value"}` plus `"exact": false` where it applies.
- In `read`, each function argument counts as outermost: `round({a} / 3, 2)`. `read` parses again to the same
  tree only within the parser's limits: the parentheses of a long chain of one operator can nest deeper than 64
  and make it longer than 1000 characters. It is for reading, not for input.
- An error found while evaluating gives the position of the operation (the operator of a binary operation, the
  name of a function) and the expression, or an excerpt of a long one, as the parser's errors do. It says what
  happened and what to do: a division by zero names the divisor as `read` writes it, and 0 to a negative power
  says it is one; a fractional exponent says there are no roots and no fractional powers; too many digits names
  the 2000-digit limit; an exponent or places out of range gives the range. Errors found after the tallies were
  read list them under `details.tallies`, since a division by zero is often a tally at 0.

Parsing:

- Chains of leading minus signs count toward the 64-level depth limit, as parentheses, function calls and
  powers do. Flat chains of one operator (`1+1+…+1`) do not: within 1000 characters they make a tree about 500
  levels deep, so whatever walks the tree (rendering `read`, evaluating) does so without recursion
  (`CalcExpression.fold`).
- `·` directly between two digits is refused as ambiguous (`1·5` may be a raised decimal point); elsewhere it
  is a multiplication.
- The comma rule needs a digit directly on both sides: `max(1k,2)` and `round({a},2)` are accepted,
  `max(1,234)` is not.
- An error at the end of the text points at the last token. The `%` error also says that `%` is not modulo.

Texts and the command line:

- The server's instructions mention `calc` in their first sentence, with an expression over tallies, and their
  last line says that number strings such as "410k" or "1,234.5" are for number arguments, while `calc`
  expressions take plain numbers without thousands separators.
- `abakus calc <expression>…` takes words starting with `-` as part of the expression (`abakus calc -2^2`), and
  drops a leading `--`. Without an expression it is a usage error. Its help, like the README, says to quote
  the expression, since `*`, `(`, `{`, `^` and spaces mean something to the shell.
- abakus 0.3.0 is the first version with `calc`.

### Consequences

- Every response and every error of `calc` is stated: when `exact` and `fraction` appear, how a rounded tally
  shows, and what each failure says and where.
- A non-zero result from half of 10^-128 up comes back as 10^-128 marked inexact rather than as an error, as
  everywhere in abakus ([ADR-0005](0005-numbers.md)).
- An agent that writes a name without its suffix is steered neither towards creating a tally nor towards
  another session's.
